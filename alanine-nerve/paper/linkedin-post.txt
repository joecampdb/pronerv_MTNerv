What if a molecular landscape were made from the molecule itself—and composition were something we could see?

I have been exploring this with two MD benchmarks: alanine dipeptide and the ten-residue chignolin variant CLN025.

Recorded conformations form translucent pathway skins that actually deform. Backbone cartoons keep the molecular identity visible. Superimposed simplicial nerves expose two distinct constructions:

• inclusion of overlapping conformational neighborhoods;
• formal composition of observed transition-route types.

That distinction is central. A → B → C is not automatically an observed direct A → C transition. Composing paths is also not yet the same as composing complete dynamical systems.

The public prototype includes 750,000 alanine frames and a reproducible 63,999-frame CLN025 subset, an illustrated paper with mathematical definitions, Blender figures and animations, and runnable source code.

The limitations are part of the work: adaptive-sampling bias, projection loss, unresolved chignolin timing metadata, unvalidated kinetics, and the difference between MD-driven visual deformation and physical simulation. The paper lays out future directions toward explicit interfaces and compositional semantics for structural bioinformatics.

I would welcome perspectives from applied category theory, molecular simulation and structural bioinformatics: which experimentally meaningful interfaces would let molecular dynamical models compose?
